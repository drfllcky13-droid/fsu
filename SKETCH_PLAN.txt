Read first: renderSketch, SHAPES/SYM/PALETTE and the sketch keydown handler in src/app.js;
all of src/ext-sketch-1.js (parseLen, solveMeas, buildWalls, DXF), ext-sketch-2.js (zoom,

Ordered by what it costs the technician. Geometry first, because a measurement that is
subtly wrong is the one failure this tool cannot recover from once the sketch is in a file.


=====================================================================
A. GEOMETRY AND MEASUREMENT — WRONG NUMBERS, FIX FIRST
=====================================================================

A1. THE MARKER'S ANCHOR IS ITS CENTRE, BUT ITS TIP IS WHAT POINTS AT THE ITEM.
    SHAPES.marker draws a tent card with a downward spike at (0.50, 0.96) of its box.
    placeAt() already honours that: tapping puts the SPIKE on the tap point.
    Everything measured does not:
      - refPoints() offers a marker as "<id>:c", and refPt() resolves :c to CORNERS.c
        = (.5,.5). So measuring FROM a marker measures from a point 46% of the marker's
        height above the spike.
      - measSheet() places a measured object with o.x = p.x - o.w/2, o.y = p.y - o.h/2 —
        it puts the CENTRE on the solved point, so the spike ends up 0.46*h below it.
      - measGeom(), measSVG() and the DXF measurement block all use objPt(o,.5,.5).
    Default marker box is 48x56 page units, so the error is 25.8 page units. At the common
    100 units = 10 ft that is 2 ft 7 in, in an exhibit that will be read off in court, and
    it is systematic: every marker, every measurement, always in the same direction.
    FIX: one anchor function, objAnchor(o) — (.5,.96) for a marker, (.5,.5) for everything
    else — used by refPt, measSheet's placement, measGeom, measSVG and the DXF block.
    Tap-place and measure-place then agree on what a marker means, which they do not today.
    MIGRATION: SKETCH_V 2 -> 3. On first open of an older sketch, every object carrying a
    .meas is re-solved from its stored tape distances, which are the record; the drawing
    follows the tape, not the other way round. An older build opening a migrated sketch
    draws the marker where the new build drew it and reports centre-based distances — i.e.
    exactly as wrong as it is today, no worse, and the .meas values are untouched.

A2. CHANGING THE SCALE DOES NOT MOVE ANYTHING THAT WAS PLACED BY MEASUREMENT.
    .meas stores real-world distances; positions are page units. scaleSheet writes
    sk.scale and re-renders. After that, measSVG still prints the stored "12 ft" against a
    line that is no longer 12 ft long, and the measurement table under the PDF disagrees
    with the drawing. Setting the scale late (which the guide actively encourages: draw
    first, set the scale from a dimension) is the normal way into this.
    FIX: after a scale change, re-solve every object with a .meas. Report how many moved.

A3. ARROW-KEY NUDGE IS THE LEAST-GUARDED MUTATION IN THE SKETCH.
    nudge() writes o.x/o.y, saveLocal(), renderSketch() and nothing else. It therefore:
      - does not push undo (see B3 for the decision);
      - does not call measFromGeometry, so nudging a measured object desynchronises the
        drawing from its measurement — the same failure as A2, one keystroke wide;
      - does not check layerLocked, so a locked layer is not locked against the keyboard;
      - does not clamp to the page, so an object can be walked off the sheet.

A4. DUPLICATE CARRIES THE ORIGINAL'S MEASUREMENT AND PHOTOGRAPH.
    Both the D shortcut and the panel's [data-odup] do
      Object.assign({},o,{id:newId(),x:o.x+24,y:o.y+24})
    and renumber markers only. So the copy, sitting 24 units away:
      - keeps .meas, and therefore claims a tape measurement that is not its own;
      - keeps .photoId, so the two objects share one photograph and deleting either one's
        photograph (photoDel) destroys it for both;
      - keeps a photopoint's number, producing two "P3" in the photograph log.
    The multi-select duplicate in ext-sketch-3 already strips both and renumbers both. The
    two single-object paths are the odd ones out.

A5. WALLS BY DIMENSION MEASURES TO THE CENTRELINE, AND DOES NOT SAY SO.
    buildWalls treats each typed length as the centreline and extends each rectangle by
    thick/2 at both ends so corners mitre. A technician taping a room reads the INSIDE
    face. Typing 16 x 12 for a room with 6 in walls therefore draws an inside dimension of
    15 ft 6 in by 11 ft 6 in — a 3 in systematic error on every wall, which then propagates
    into anything measured off a wall corner (refPoints offers named wall corners as fixed
    points, so it propagates into the measurement record, not just the picture).
    FIX: a "Measured to" choice on the Room tab — inside face (default, it is what a tape
    gives you), centreline, outside face — converting to the centreline before building.
    Run-of-walls keeps the centreline, since a run has no consistent inside.

A6. THE SCALE SHEET'S "REAL LENGTH" FIELD USES parseFloat, NOT parseLen.
    Typing 12'6" to declare a scale silently sets 12. Every length in the sketch then
    carries a 4% error. parseLen exists and is used everywhere else.

A7. measure() FORMATS A NEGATIVE FEET VALUE WRONG.
    Math.floor(-1.5) = -2, then the remainder rounds to 6 in, printing -2' 6" for -1' 6".
    Only reachable through a negative dimension, but it is one line.


=====================================================================
B. THE UNDO STACK MUST NOT LIE
=====================================================================

B1. THE SNAPSHOT IS sk.objs AND NOTHING ELSE.
    snapOf() is JSON.stringify(sk.objs). So none of this can be undone: adding a layer,
    renaming one, locking one, deleting one, setting or clearing the scale, flipping the
    page to portrait, toggling measurement lines. Worse, deleting a layer DOES push undo,
    so the button lights up and the user presses it: the objects get their old .lay back,
    the layer itself does not come back, layerOf() silently falls through to layers[0], and
    the layer's name is gone for good. The undo stack claims to have restored something it
    has not. That is the failure mode the brief names.
    FIX: snapshot {objs, layers, scale, portrait, showMeas}. Keep reading the old
    array-shaped snapshots out of localStorage so a reload mid-shift does not lose history.

B2. UNDO COVERAGE AUDIT — every path that mutates a sketch.
    Has undo, correct:   panel delete, panel duplicate, Delete key, D key, ink finish,
                         poly finish, add object, template, walls, measurement place and
                         clear, typed size/rotation/fill, ink colour, rotate buttons,
                         renumber, layer move drag, object move/resize/rotate drag, vertex
                         drag, add/remove vertex, style paste, multi colour/duplicate/
                         delete, reorder (forward/back/up/down), move to layer.
    NO undo, mutates the record:
        arrow-key nudge                   -> B3
        layer rename                      -> B1 covers it once layers are in the snapshot
        layer lock/unlock                 -> B1
        scale set / "not to scale"        -> B1 + A2
        portrait/landscape flip           -> B1
        rotation lock toggle ([data-orotlock])  -> B1 (it is an object field; add pushUndo)
        backdrop add/move/fit/brightness  -> left alone, see NOT DOING
    Wrong shape of undo: the duplicate paths (A4) push undo and then write a bad copy.

B3. WHAT TO DO ABOUT THE NUDGE. The current answer (no undo, because 40 taps would flush a
    40-deep history) trades one real problem for a worse one: the arrow keys are the only
    way to mutate the drawing that undo cannot reach, and they are exactly what a technician
    uses on the desktop machine to tidy a sketch before export. The right answer is not "no
    undo" or "undo per keystroke" but COALESCE: the first nudge of a run pushes one
    snapshot, and subsequent nudges of the same object within 1.2 s of the last one do not.
    A run of 40 taps then costs one entry and undoes as one gesture, which is what the user
    means by "undo that". Cheap: one module-level {id,t} and a timestamp compare.


=====================================================================
C. LAYERS
=====================================================================

C1. NEW OBJECTS GO INTO A LOCKED LAYER WITHOUT COMPLAINT.
    Five call sites compute the same target layer:
      target = (curLayer && ls.some(l=>l.id===curLayer)) ? curLayer : ls[ls.length-1].id
    (addObj, wallSheet, polyFinish, applyTemplate, inkFinish). None checks .locked. Lock a
    layer, place a marker: it lands, gets selected, and then refuses to move — "Layer is
    locked" — with no way to tell why it went there. One shared targetLayer(sk) that skips
    locked layers, and says so when every layer is locked.

C2. multiDelete IGNORES LOCKS. The marquee excludes locked objects and tap-to-add checks
    the lock, but if a layer is locked AFTER objects are in the selection, Delete removes
    them anyway. Filter in multiDelete.

C3. LAYER DELETE re-homes objects into ls.find(x=>x.id!==L.id) — the first other layer,
    which may not be the one next to it, and the count shown in the button ("3 objects move
    out") does not say where they go. Say where.


=====================================================================
D. NOT DOING, AND WHY
=====================================================================

- NOT restructuring the three ext-sketch files into a module. They are append-only patches
  over app.js with a hook convention (sketchExtraClick/extraPointerDown chains) that works
  and is tested. RECOMMENDATION, not a change: the pointer-handler chain
  (extraPointerDown3 || extraPointerDown2 || extraPointerDown) is the one place where the
  ordering is load-bearing and invisible; if a fourth tool is ever added it should become a
  registered list with named priorities rather than a fourth || term.
- NOT changing the photopoint's anchor. Its symbol is a camera on a tripod; unlike a
  marker it denotes a viewpoint, not a point on the ground, and moving its anchor would
  migrate every photograph-log position for no gain in the record.
- NOT making snapEdges rotation-aware. It snaps unrotated bounding boxes, so a rotated wall
  snaps to the wrong edges. Alt already disables it and the grid covers the common case;
  doing it properly means separating-axis work on every object every pointermove, which is
  the wrong trade on an iPad.
- NOT touching the sync layer, the PDF vector path, or the backdrop editor's undo. The
  backdrop is not part of the measured record and its editor is live-preview by design.
- NOT doing large-sketch performance work. renderSketch rebuilds the whole SVG string on
  every change and patchSketchView diffs innerHTML; a 300-object sketch redraws in a few
  ms and the drag paths already bypass it by patching single <g> elements. Measured before
  guessed: nothing here is slow enough to justify the risk. RECOMMENDATION if it ever is:
  the drag paths are already the right pattern, extend it to the nudge and colour paths.
- NOT adding new tools (curved walls, area calculation, bearings from magnetic north).
  Missing features cost less than wrong ones.

STILL OPEN, NOT FIXED
- model.spec.js still skips markers, photopoints, moves, resizes and layer operations. The
  setTimeout in addObj (syncMarker/syncPhoto write to other records a tick later) is why;
  the model would need to await a frame after every add. sketch-geom.spec.js now covers
  those paths directly instead, which is the cheaper half of the job, but the random-action
  model is the one that finds what nobody thought of and it should be extended.
- The backdrop editor (position, fit, brightness, saturation, opacity) mutates the sketch
  with no undo at all. Out of scope here: it is not part of the measured record.
- snapEdges is not rotation-aware (D).
- DXF writes $INSUNITS into an AC1009 (R12) header, where it is not a defined variable.
  Every reader tried ignores it rather than failing, and the drawing is in real units
  regardless, so it is noise rather than a fault.
